# FyndEt — Privacy, Safety and Acceptable Use

## Guiding standard
An email address can identify a person. FyndEt must not convert public fragments into a harassment, stalking or indiscriminate profiling product. Being technically retrievable does **not** by itself establish a lawful basis for processing or redistribution.

## Allowed use examples (subject to review)
- User examines and verifies their own address.
- Employer/organisation examines domains and accounts it demonstrably controls within a legitimate scope.
- Approved professional investigator conducts a proportionate inquiry under documented authority and applicable law.

## Prohibited use
Doxxing, stalking, harassment, discrimination, mass marketing list building, unauthorised employee vetting, credential attacks, privacy invasions, bulk address enumeration, bypassing sign-in/reset/registration protections, harvesting social media contrary to terms, scraping behind authentication, distributing passwords or sensitive breach materials.

## Controls
- Verify account holders; for sensitive sources, verify control of the subject email or organisational domain, or capture reviewed legal authority.
- Restrict all investigations by purpose, jurisdiction, source licence and data subject safeguards.
- Apply strict Free-tier output minimisation and request limits; do not expose sensitive breach details to anonymous visitors.
- Maintain source provenance and right-to-redisclose metadata; suppress sensitive personal categories by default.
- Document retention, evidence expiry, deletion and access logging. Restrict raw evidence to authorised personnel.
- Create data subject rights flow for access, correction, objection, deletion and dispute; support report takedown and investigation halt.
- Provide clear consent/notice language where required; do not assert a consent checkbox cures unlawful collection.
- Detect abusive search patterns, cross-tenant attacks, scraping and repeated searches for unrelated individuals; freeze access pending investigation.
- Review POPIA in South Africa, GDPR where applicable, and contractual terms of every provider with legal counsel before release.
- Complete DPIA/privacy impact assessment, threat model and breach response runbook before production.
- Use an administrator exception pathway only with documented, role-limited, reviewable approvals; avoid silent bypasses.

## Breach result requirements
Only compliant providers. Prefer notification metadata and guidance over individual raw data. Domain-wide matching must require verified domain control or independently validated contractual authority. Never reveal passwords, hashes, tokens, security answers or private recovered content.

## Evidence semantics
Do not infer an account exists from a service's lack of error response. Distinguish 'observed publicly', 'possible association' and 'not searched'. Show confidence as evidence strength rather than certainty of identity. Provide correction and source appeal options.

## Go-live gate
Written privacy assessment, lawful basis and use matrix, vetted providers, abuse test plan, platform terms, secure retention defaults, breach notification process and signed security acceptance.
